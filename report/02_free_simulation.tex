\section{The Free Simulation}

While explicit simulations effectively prevent infinite stuttering,
they impose a substantial proof burden.
Every stuttering transition requires exhibiting a strictly decreasing
measure in a well-founded order.
Constructing such a measure can be difficult in practice,
particularly when compiler optimizations rearrange loop structures.
In such scenarios,
finding a global metric that strictly decreases on every asynchronous step
while remaining invariant across complex control transfers
is often impractical.

To overcome this rigidity,
we adapt the \emph{Stuttering for Free} framework
introduced by \textcite{cho-23-stuttering}.
We extend their foundational technique to accommodate
skewed simulation relations.
We have formalized and verified all underlying theoretical results
within our Rocq development.

\subsection{Skewed Implicit Simulation}

A possible approach to eliminating explicit stuttering measures
is to integrate inductive and coinductive reasoning into a single relation.
In this formulation,
finite sequences of asynchronous execution steps are justified inductively,
whereas mutual progress steps are justified coinductively.
This family of relations is called
\emph{implicit simulations}.
We define a skewed implicit simulation relation,
denoted $\isim{t}{s}{\Phi}$,
whose inference rules are presented in \cref{fig:implicit-sim}.

To combine inductive and coinductive reasoning within the same definition,
the relation $\isim{t}{s}{\Phi}$ is defined as the greatest fixed point%
\footnote{The relationship between greatest fixed points and coinduction
  follows from the Knaster--Tarski theorem;
  see \cites{pous-16-cawu}{schafer-17-tower} for comprehensive treatments.}
of an inductive operator.
The rules \textsc{IRelated},
\textsc{ISourceStuck},
\textsc{ISourceSteps},
and \textsc{ITargetSteps} serve as inductive constructors,
while the rule \textsc{IBothSteps} acts as the coinductive constructor.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \begin{align*}
    &
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\bfinal{\Phi}{t}{s}}
      \infer1[\textsc{IRelated}]{\isim{t}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
    &
    &
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\stuck{P_s}{s}}
      \infer1[\textsc{ISourceStuck}]{\isim{t}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{align*}
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\step{P_s}{s}{s'}}
      \hypo{\isim{t}{s'}{\Phi}}
      \infer2[\textsc{ISourceSteps}]{\isim{t}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\canp{P_t}{t}}
      \hypo{\forall t', \step{P_t}{t}{t'} \implies \isim{t'}{s}{\Phi}}
      \infer2[\textsc{ITargetSteps}]{\isim{t}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\canp{P_t}{t}}
      \hypo{\forall t',
        \step{P_t}{t}{t'} \implies
        \exists s', \step{P_s}{s}{s'} \land \isim{t'}{s'}{\Phi}}
      \infer[coind]2[\textsc{IBothSteps}]{\isim{t}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \caption{Derivation rules of the \emph{skewed implicit simulation}.}
  \label{fig:implicit-sim}
\end{figure}

The rule \textsc{IRelated} characterizes terminating executions.
Instead of demanding syntactic equality between terminal values,
it is parameterized by a postcondition $\Phi$
that relates the final values and the concluding memory states
of the target and source programs.
The predicate $\textsf{both\_final}$ is defined as follows:
\begin{gather*}
  \Phi :
  \textsf{val}_t \to
  \textsf{val}_s \to
  \textsf{mem} \to
  \textsf{mem} \to
  \textsf{Prop}
  \\
  \begin{aligned}
    \bfinal{\Phi}{(t, m_t)}{(s, m_s)} \coloneqq
    \exists v_t v_s,\;
    &\isval{t} = \Some{v_t} \land \isval{s} = \Some{v_s} \land {} \\
    &\Phi(v_t, v_s, m_t, m_s)
  \end{aligned}
\end{gather*}
The assertion $\bfinal{\Phi}{t}{s}$
holds when both target and source configurations have reached final states,
producing values $v_t, v_s$ and memories $m_t, m_s$
that satisfy $\Phi$.
This parameterization enables the simulation to enforce
relational invariants on memories at the end of execution.

The rule \textsc{ISourceStuck} introduces more asymmetry to the relation.
When the source program encounters undefined behavior
it enters a stuck state in a non-final configuration.
In this case,
\textsc{ISourceStuck} immediately validates the simulation,
leaving the target program free to perform arbitrary transitions or diverge.
This asymmetry allows compiler transformations
that rely on the absence of undefined behavior in well-formed source programs
to eliminate redundant runtime checks.

Stuttering is soundly handled without an explicit decreasing measure.
Because \textsc{ISourceSteps} and \textsc{ITargetSteps} are inductive
constructors,
they permit only finitely many consecutive asynchronous transitions.
The coinductive constructor \textsc{IBothSteps} requires
both programs to execute a synchronized step,
to remove the inductive proof obligation
and enable unbounded mutual progress.

\subsection{Step-Indexed Free Simulation}

While the implicit simulation eliminates manual stuttering measures,
it remains restrictive:
the coinductive rule \textsc{IBothSteps}
demands that both programs step at the exact moment
when coinductive reasoning is resumed.
Removing this lock-step synchronization requirement
leads to smaller invariants
and more direct verification proofs~\parencite{cho-23-stuttering}.
This decoupling is the core idea developed in the \emph{Stuttering for Free} framework.

The \emph{free simulation},
denoted $\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}$,
overcomes this limitation by decoupling synchronous execution steps
from coinductive progress.
The relation is parameterized by two well-founded index domains,
$(J, \prec_J)$ for the target side
and $(I, \prec_I)$ for the source side.
These domains track the progress accumulated by each side
since the previous coinductive step.
The formal inference rules of the skewed free simulation
are presented in \cref{fig:free-sim}.
Like the implicit simulation,
it accommodates arbitrary postconditions $\Phi$
and uses the rule \textsc{FSourceStuck}
to exploit any undefined behavior in the source.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \begin{align*}
    &
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\bfinal{\Phi}{t}{s}}
      \infer1[\textsc{FRelated}]{\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
    &
    &
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\stuck{P_s}{s}}
      \infer1[\textsc{FSourceStuck}]{\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{align*}
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\step{P_s}{s}{s'}}
      \hypo{\fsim{t}{j}{i'}{s'}{\Phi}}
      \infer2[\textsc{FSourceSteps}]{\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\canp{P_t}{t}}
      \hypo{\forall t',
        \step{P_t}{t}{t'} \implies
        \exists j', \fsim{t'}{j'}{i}{s}{\Phi}}
      \infer2[\textsc{FTargetSteps}]{\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{j' \prec_J j}
      \hypo{i' \prec_I i}
      \hypo{\fsim{t}{j'}{i'}{s}{\Phi}}
      \infer[coind]3[\textsc{FProgress}]{\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \caption{Derivation rules of the \emph{skewed free simulation}.}
  \label{fig:free-sim}
\end{figure}

The design of the free simulation relies on a fundamental shift in perspective
regarding indices.
In an explicit simulation,
an index represents an upper bound on \emph{future} stuttering steps.
In the free simulation,
indices record \emph{past} progress
accumulated since the previous coinductive step.

The source and target programs take transitions asynchronously
via the inductive rules \textsc{FTargetSteps} and \textsc{FSourceSteps}.
Whenever a program takes a step,
its corresponding step index is updated ($j'$ or $i'$).
We note that $j'$ and $i'$ are not required to be
immediate successors of $j$ and $i$.
Instead the user is free to select any new index,
smaller or larger.

This flexibility does not compromise soundness.
Indeed,
the only rule that enables coinductive reasoning is \textsc{FProgress}.
This rule requires both indices to strictly decrease,
from $j$ down to $j' \prec_J j$
and from $i$ down to $i' \prec_I i$.
Because the relations $\prec_J$ and $\prec_I$ are well-founded,
establishing a simulation between non-terminating executions
demands that \textsc{FProgress} be applied infinitely often,
which in turn requires indices to be repeatedly increased
through \textsc{FTargetSteps} and \textsc{FSourceSteps}.
This structure decouples stepping from coinductive synchronization,
allowing multiple asynchronous transitions on either side to be matched flexibly.

Moreover, performing coinductive reasoning over the free simulation
is equivalent to the following principle:
to prove $\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}$,
one may assume the coinductive hypothesis:
\begin{equation*}
  \forall j' \succ_J j,\; \forall i' \succ_I i,\quad
  \fsim{t}{j'}{i'}{s}{\Phi}.
\end{equation*}
Intuitively,
this principle allows one to establish that two programs simulate each other
by assuming that the simulation relation already holds for the current
states if they are reached after progress on both sides.
A concrete example illustrating step-indexed reasoning in the free simulation
is presented in \cref{app:free-sim-example}.

\subsection{Properties}

Proving a free simulation for smaller indices
implies that it holds for larger indices.
This monotonicity property is valid not only
as an external lemma,
but also within coinductive proofs.
This property is captured by index monotonicity:
\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity of Free Simulation]
  \label{lem:fsim-mono}
  If $\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}$ holds,
  then for all indices $j \prec_J j'$ and $i \prec_I i'$,
  and any weaker postcondition $\Phi \implies \Phi'$,
  the simulation $\fsim{t}{j'}{i'}{s}{\Phi'}$ holds.
\end{lemma}
Next, the free simulation is sound with respect to the skewed behavioral refinement:
\begin{theorem}[Soundness of Free Simulation]
  \label{thm:fsim-soundness}
  Let $P_t$ and $P_s$ be programs.
  Let $t$ be the initial state of $P_t$ and
  $s$ be the initial state of $P_s$.
  If $\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{=}$ holds for some indices $j$ and $i$,
  then $P_t \slantsqsubseteq P_s$.
\end{theorem}
In addition to soundness,
we established the formal equivalence
among the three simulation frameworks discussed:
the free simulation (defined in \cref{fig:free-sim}),
the implicit simulation (defined in \cref{fig:implicit-sim}),
and a skewed version of the explicit simulation
(defined in \cref{fig:explicit-sim} augmented with a stuck-source rule).
This equivalence is established by chaining cyclic implications:
\begin{equation*}
  \text{Explicit Simulation}
  \overset{\text{\incirc{1}}}{\implies}
  \text{Implicit Simulation}
  \overset{\text{\incirc{2}}}{\implies}
  \text{Free Simulation}
  \overset{\text{\incirc{3}}}{\implies}
  \text{Explicit Simulation}
\end{equation*}
The implications \incirc{1} and \incirc{2}
proceed directly by simulation coinduction.
The last implication \incirc{3} is the most intricate:
it requires synthesizing a well-founded stuttering measure
from the derivation tree of the free simulation.
A direct consequence of this equivalence is the index irrelevance theorem:
\begin{theorem}[Index Irrelevance]
  \label{thm:index-irrel}
  Let $(J, \prec_J)$ and $(I, \prec_I)$ be well-founded relations without
  isolated elements%
  \footnote{An element is isolated
    if it has no predecessors or successors in the relation.}.
  If there exist indices $j \in J$ and $i \in I$
  such that $\fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}$,
  then for any other well-founded structures
  $(J', \prec_{J'})$ and $(I', \prec_{I'})$,
  and any indices $j' \in J'$ and $i' \in I'$,
  the relation $\fsim{t}{j'}{i'}{s}{\Phi}$ holds.
\end{theorem}
Our statement refines the index irrelevance theorem
formulated by \textcite{cho-23-stuttering}.
Their original formulation,
phrased in terms of ordinals,
implicitly assumes that every element participates in the order.
However,
isolated elements could cause \textsc{FProgress}
transitions to be uninhabited,
preventing the equivalence.
This distinction was identified
during our Rocq formalization of the metatheory of the free simulation.

This theorem implies that any free simulation derivation
can be carried out using the two-element boolean order
$\mathbb{B} = \{\false \prec \true\}$:
\begin{equation*}
  (\exists j \in J, i \in I,\; \fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi})
  \iff
  (\exists j, i \in \mathbb{B},\; \fsim{t}{j}{i}{s}{\Phi}).
\end{equation*}
Under the boolean order,
$\textsf{false}$ indicates that a program has not yet taken a step,
whereas $\textsf{true}$ signals that progress has occurred.
This mean that,
in theory,
the free simulation proofs
does not strictly require tracking
the exact step counts of individual programs,
but only whether progress has occurred
since the last coinductive step.
Nevertheless,
in the remainder of our work,
we adopt the natural numbers $(\mathbb{N}, <)$
as our index domain.
Indeed,
natural number indices correspond directly
to the number of steps executed during symbolic evaluation,
and they provide some intuition that facilitates
the interactive verification in Rocq.
