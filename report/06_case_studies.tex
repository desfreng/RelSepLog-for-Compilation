\section{Case Studies}
\label{sec:case-studies}

To evaluate the relational separation logic developed in this work,
we present three case studies:
a local transformation exploiting undefined behavior,
a loop transformation hoisting an invariant load,
and a control-flow optimization eliminating redundant jump chains.

\subsection{Undefined Behavior Exploitation}
\label{sec:case-study-ub}

We begin with a local program transformation that eliminates a redundant
zero-check after an arithmetic division.
Consider the source and target code fragments in \cref{fig:case-study-ub}.

\begin{figure}[htp]
  \centering
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{array}{r@{\;\colon\;}l c r@{\;\colon\;}l}
      \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{Source Program}}
      & \hspace{2cm}
      & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{Target Program}}
      \\
      \midrule
      1 & \Iop{r_1}{\Odiv}{[r_n, r_d]}{2}
      & \quad\implies\quad
      & 1 & \Iop{r_1}{\Odiv}{[r_n, r_d]}{2}
      \\
      2 & \Iop{r_b}{\Oeqz}{[r_d]}{3}
      &
      & 2 & \Iop{r_b}{\Oimmbool{\false}}{}{3}
      \\
      3 & \dots
      &
      & 3 & \dots
    \end{array}
  \end{equation*}
  \caption{Exploiting division undefined behavior to simplify a zero test.}
  \label{fig:case-study-ub}
\end{figure}

In the source program,
instruction 1 divides the content of $r_n$ by $r_d$,
storing the quotient in $r_1$.
Instruction 2 tests whether the divisor $r_d$ is zero via $\Oeqz$,
storing the resulting boolean in $r_b$.
In the target program,
instruction 2 replaces this test with an immediate assignment of $\false$.

The soundness of this transformation relies on the asymmetric nature of our
simulation relation.
When reasoning about instruction 1,
we apply the rule \textsc{Source-Op-Exploit}.
Because stuck source executions satisfy the simulation relation by
\textsc{FSourceStuck},
the rule provides the hypothesis that the operation succeeds:
$\evalop{\Odiv}{[\rho_s(r_n), \rho_s(r_d)]} = \Some{v}$.
Under our operational semantics,
division succeeds only when the divisor $\rho_s(r_d) = \VInt{z_d}$
satisfies $z_d \neq 0$.
Assuming that target and source register banks agree initially,
the target division executes identically,
advancing both programs to $pc = 2$.

At program point 2,
the source program tests register $r_d$ for equality to zero.
Because $\getreg{r_d}{\rho_s} = \VInt{z_d}$ with $z_d \neq 0$,
this evaluation deterministically yields $\false$.
In the target program,
the immediate load executes via \textsc{Target-Op}
and writes $\false$ into $r_b$ without inspecting $r_d$.
Both programs reach $pc = 3$ in matching register and memory states,
establishing the soundness of this transformation.

\subsection{Load Hoisting}
\label{sec:case-study-hoisting}

We next examine a transformation hoisting a load out of a loop.
\Cref{fig:fact-programs} displays two versions of a factorial function
$\textsf{fact}(n, addr)$.
The function initializes the memory cell at $addr$ to $1$,
and computes the factorial of $n$.

\begin{figure}[htp]
  \centering
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{array}{r@{\;\colon\;}l c r@{\;\colon\;}l}
      \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{Source Function } (\textsf{fact\_slow})}
      & \hspace{2cm}
      & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{Target Function } (\textsf{fact\_fast})}
      \\
      \midrule
      0 & \Iop{r_{res}}{\Oimmint{1}}{}{1}
      &
      & 0 & \Iop{r_{res}}{\Oimmint{1}}{}{1}
      \\
      1 & \Istore{r_{addr}}{r_{res}}{2}
      &
      & 1 & \Istore{r_{addr}}{r_{res}}{2}
      \\
      \multicolumn{2}{c}{}
      &
      & 2 & \Iop{r_{one}}{\Oimmint{1}}{}{3}
      \\
      2 & \Iop{r_{cond}}{\Oeqz}{[r_n]}{3}
      &
      & 3 & \Iop{r_{cond}}{\Oeqz}{[r_n]}{4}
      \\
      3 & \Icond{r_{cond}}{7}{4}
      &
      & 4 & \Icond{r_{cond}}{7}{5}
      \\
      4 & \Iop{r_{res}}{\Omul}{[r_{res}, r_n]}{5}
      &
      & 5 & \Iop{r_{res}}{\Omul}{[r_{res}, r_n]}{6}
      \\
      5 & \Iload{r_{one}}{r_{addr}}{6}
      &
      & \multicolumn{2}{c}{}
      \\
      6 & \Iop{r_n}{\Osub}{[r_n, r_{one}]}{2}
      &
      & 6 & \Iop{r_n}{\Osub}{[r_n, r_{one}]}{3}
      \\
      7 & \Iret{r_{res}}
      &
      & 7 & \Iret{r_{res}}
    \end{array}
  \end{equation*}
  \caption{Source and target factorial functions illustrating load hoisting.}
  \label{fig:fact-programs}
\end{figure}

In the unoptimized function \textsf{fact\_slow},
the loop body performs a redundant heap load at $pc = 5$ on each iteration,
reading the constant $1$ stored at the address in $r_{addr}$ to decrement $r_n$.
The optimized function \textsf{fact\_fast} hoists the load into the prelude.
It further replaces the memory access with an immediate assignment of $1$
into register $r_{one}$.

To prove the soundness of this transformation,
we prove the following relational Hoare quadruple for all heap bijections $I$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:hoisting-hoare}
  \hoareq{\sameargsf{I}}{\textsf{fact\_fast}}{\textsf{fact\_slow}}{\samerf{I}}
\end{equation}
where $\sameargsf{I}$ and $\samerf{I}$
relate input arguments and return values under an extendable heap bijection:
\begin{align*}
  \sameargs{I}{\vec{v}_t}{\vec{v}_s}
  & \coloneqq
    \purel{\textsf{Forall2}(\sameval{I}{}{}, \vec{v}_t, \vec{v}_s)}
    \sep \meminj{I}{\emptyset}
  \\
  \samer{I}{v_t}{v_s}
  & \coloneqq
    \exists I',\;
    \purel{I \subseteq I'} \sep
    \purel{\sameval{I'}{v_t}{v_s}} \sep
    \meminj{I'}{\emptyset}
\end{align*}
The precondition $\sameargs{I}{\vec{v}_t}{\vec{v}_s}$
asserts that input arguments are pairwise related under $I$,
alongside a complete ($E = \emptyset$) memory injection $\meminj{I}{\emptyset}$.
The postcondition $\samer{I}{v_t}{v_s}$ guarantees that return values
are related under an extended bijection $I' \supseteq I$,
with the memory injection $\meminj{I'}{\emptyset}$ fully restored.
Allowing the bijection to grow monotonically accounts for any fresh memory
allocations performed during execution.

The proof of \cref{eq:hoisting-hoare} begins by stepping through the prelude.
Both programs initialize $r_{res}$ to $1$.
Applying \textsc{Source-Store-Exploit} extracts $(l_t, l_s)$ from
$\meminj{I}{\emptyset}$,
yielding $\ptrs{l_s}{\VInt{1}}$ and advancing the source to $pc_s = 2$.
Applying \textsc{Target-Store} then executes the target store,
producing $\ptrt{l_t}{\VInt{1}}$.
Finally,
the target initializes $r_{one}$ via \textsc{Target-Op}
and reaches $pc_t = 3$,
establishing the following loop invariant:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The target program is executing \textsf{fact\_fast}
        at program counter $pc_t = 3$ with register bank $\rho_t$.
  \item The source program is executing \textsf{fact\_slow}
        at program counter $pc_s = 2$ with register bank $\rho_s$.
  \item The register banks agree on the counter and accumulator:
        $\sameval{I}{\rho_t(r_n)}{\rho_s(r_n)}$
        and $\sameval{I}{\rho_t(r_{res})}{\rho_s(r_{res})}$.
  \item The registers $r_{addr}$ hold related pointer values:
        $\rho_t(r_{addr}) = \VPtr{l_t}$
        and $\rho_s(r_{addr}) = \VPtr{l_s}$
        with $(l_t, l_s) \in I$.
  \item The target register $r_{one}$ holds the constant $\VInt{1}$
        in $\rho_t$.
  \item The memory state satisfies
        $\ptrt{l_t}{\VInt{1}} \sep \ptrs{l_s}{\VInt{1}} \sep
        \meminj{I}{\{ (l_t, l_s) \}}$,
        isolating the hoisted cell $(l_t, l_s)$
        while the remaining heap is governed by the injection.
\end{itemize}

Because the programs contain a loop and may diverge (when $n < 0$)
the proof proceeds by coinduction.
Our logic framework establishes semantic equivalence regardless of
termination.
Evaluating the zero-test $\Oeqz(r_n) = b$
synchronously in both programs yields two cases:
\begin{description}
  \item[Base case ({\normalfont $b = \true$}):]
        Both programs branch to the return instruction at $pc = 7$.
        The points-to assertions
        $\ptrt{l_t}{\VInt{1}} \sep \ptrs{l_s}{\VInt{1}}$
        are returned to the memory injection via the reintegration lemma,
        restoring $\meminj{I}{\emptyset}$.
        Both programs execute $\Iret{r_{res}}$,
        satisfying the postcondition $\samerf{I}$ with $I' = I$.

  \item[Step case ({\normalfont $b = \false$}):]
        Both programs execute the multiplication
        $r_{res} \coloneqq r_{res} \times r_n$.
        At source $pc_s = 5$,
        the source load $r_{one} \coloneqq {!r_{addr}}$ executes via
        \textsc{Source-Load}
        directly from $\ptrs{l_s}{\VInt{1}}$
        without requiring memory exploitation.
        Both programs then decrement $r_n \coloneqq r_n - r_{one}$,
        preserving register agreement,
        and branch back to the loop headers ($pc_t = 3$ and $pc_s = 2$).
        This re-establishes the invariant,
        closing the case by the coinductive hypothesis.
\end{description}

\subsection{Nop Tunneling}
\label{sec:case-study-nop}

We now consider an optimization on arbitrary control-flow graphs present in
CompCert~\parencite{leroy-09-backend}:
nop tunneling.
Intermediate compiler passes frequently introduce redundant jump
instructions ($\Inop{pc}$).
A sequence of consecutive jumps forms a \emph{nop chain}:
\begin{equation*}
  pc_0 \to pc_1 \to pc_2 \to \dots \to pc_k \to pc_{\text{final}}
\end{equation*}
where each node $pc_m$ ($0 \leqslant m < k$) contains $\Inop{pc_{m+1}}$,
and $pc_{\text{final}}$ is the first non-nop instruction.
Nop tunneling short-circuits these chains by redirecting branch targets
directly to $pc_{\text{final}}$,
reducing instruction count and eliminating superfluous jump overhead.

The transformation is defined in Rocq over an arbitrary \muRTL{} function $f$.
To ensure termination,
even in the presence of cyclic jump chains
(such as an infinite loop $pc \colon \Inop{pc}$),
the computation of the final successor of an instruction at $pc$
is parameterized by a recursion depth $d \in \mathbb{N}$:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{aligned}
    \findnextpc{0}{f}{pc}
    & \coloneqq pc \\
    \findnextpc{1 + d}{f}{pc}
    & \coloneqq
      \begin{cases}
        \findnextpc{d}{f}{pc'}
        & \text{if } \pcatis{f}{pc}{\Inop{pc'}}
        \\
        pc
        & \text{otherwise}
      \end{cases}
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The optimization $\tunnelnop{d}{f}$ maps $\findnextpcf$
over every instruction in the control-flow graph of $f$,
redirecting each successor label $pc$ to
$\findnextpc{d}{f}{pc}$.
The function entry point is updated in the same manner.
To establish the soundness of nop tunneling for an arbitrary function $f$,
we prove the following relational Hoare quadruple for all depths $d$
and heap bijections $I$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nop-hoare}
  \hoareq{\sameargsf{I}}{\tunnelnop{d}{f}}{f}{\samerf{I}}
\end{equation}
The proof relies on a coinductive simulation invariant.
Given a fixed initial fuel $depth$,
the invariant asserts that at any step of execution:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The target program is executing the optimized function
        $\tunnelnop{depth}{f}$ at program counter $pc_t$
        with register bank $\rho_t$.
  \item The source program is executing the original function $f$
        at program counter $pc_s$ with register bank $\rho_s$.
  \item The target program counter is the tunneled successor of the source
        program counter with remaining fuel $d$:
        $pc_t = \findnextpc{d}{f}{pc_s}$.
  \item The register banks agree on all registers:
        $\forall r,\; \sameval{I}{\rho_t(r)}{\rho_s(r)}$.
  \item The memory heaps are fully synchronized under a monotone bijection $I$:
        $\meminj{I}{\emptyset}$.
\end{itemize}

The proof of \cref{eq:nop-hoare} proceeds by coinduction
followed by an inner induction on the remaining fuel $d$:
\begin{description}
  \item[Base case ($d = 0$):]
        When fuel is exhausted,
        $\findnextpc{0}{f}{pc_s} = pc_s$,
        so $pc_t = pc_s$.
        The target and source are positioned at identical program points.
        By case analysis on the instruction at $pc_s$,
        both programs progress in lockstep for each instruction kind.
        The next target program counter is the redirected label
        $\findnextpc{depth}{f}{pc_s'}$,
        which re-establishes the invariant with full fuel $d = depth$.
        The coinductive hypothesis closes this case.

  \item[Inductive step ($d > 0$):]
        We inspect the source instruction at $pc_s$:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item If $pc_s$ maps to a jump $\Inop{pc_s'}$,
                then $\findnextpc{1 + d'}{f}{pc_s} =
                \findnextpc{d'}{f}{pc_s'}$.
                The source executes the jump via \textsc{Source-Nop},
                advancing to $pc_s'$.
                The target remains at $pc_t$.
                The inner induction hypothesis on $d'$ concludes this case.

          \item If $pc_s$ does not refer to a jump instruction,
                $\findnextpc{1 + d'}{f}{pc_s} = pc_s$,
                so $pc_t = pc_s$.
                As in the base case,
                target and source progress in lockstep,
                resetting fuel to $depth$ for successors.
                The coinductive hypothesis closes this case.
        \end{itemize}
\end{description}

When verifying function call instructions,
the proof applies the synchronous \textsc{Both-Call} rule.
Because the coinductive invariant is parameterized
over arbitrary \muRTL{} functions,
it can be instantiated directly for the callee.
Therefore,
the relational Hoare quadruple required by \textsc{Both-Call}
is discharged without additional lemmas.
This illustrates the modularity of our relational separation logic:
function-level optimizations are verified in isolation
and compose cleanly across whole-program call graphs.
